# Public source and evidence boundaries

This file contains only public references. It does not depend on a local research workspace. Read only the section relevant to the current question.

## What these sources can support

| Topic | Public source | Permitted use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intimate-partner violence and controlling behavior | [WHO: Violence against women](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/violence-against-women) | Recognize reported violence or controlling conduct as a safety issue and prioritize immediate safety over ordinary relationship coaching. | Do not diagnose a person, predict an individual's future behavior, or prescribe one universal leaving plan. |
| Acute suicide risk | [WHO: Suicide](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/suicide) | Treat an expressed immediate plan, available means, an attempt, or ingestion as urgent and direct the user to current local emergency help and in-person support. | Do not use a fixed hotline without checking location and currency; do not substitute relationship negotiation for emergency care. |
| Romance scams and money requests | [U.S. FTC: What to know about romance scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams) | Pause new transfers or credential sharing and use contact details the user independently obtains from the relevant bank, platform, or authority. | Warning signs do not prove a particular person is a criminal; do not promise recovery, assign a crime, or copy U.S. procedures to another jurisdiction. |
| Account security | [CISA: Secure Our World](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world) | Recommend official account-recovery channels, strong unique passwords, and multifactor authentication when an account may be exposed. | Do not provide intrusion, surveillance, credential theft, or retaliation instructions. Product steps must be checked against the current official service documentation. |
| Privacy and forced disclosure of sexual orientation or gender identity | [UN Human Rights Council report A/HRC/41/45](https://docs.un.org/en/A/HRC/41/45) | Preserve the person's control over disclosure and treat threatened outing as a privacy and safety concern. | This is a normative human-rights source, not individualized legal advice or evidence for a best coming-out method. Local law and support channels require current jurisdiction-specific verification. |

## General evidence rules

- Observable messages and behavior do not establish private motives.
- A mechanism or group-level association does not prove that a specific dating tactic will work.
- Do not invent success rates, universal waiting periods, contact frequencies, observation counts, or diagnostic scores.
- A public safety reference can support conservative routing; it does not establish the effectiveness of a script or guarantee a real-world outcome.
- Laws, emergency numbers, reporting procedures, platform rules, and institutional duties change by place and time. Verify current official sources before giving specifics.
- If a source is unavailable or does not directly support a claim, narrow the claim instead of filling the gap with confidence.
